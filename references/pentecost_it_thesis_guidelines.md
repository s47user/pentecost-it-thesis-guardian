# Pentecost University IT Department Thesis Guidelines & Specification

This reference document establishes the academic submission regulations, formatting constraints, and chapter blueprints established by the Department of Information Technology, Faculty of Engineering, Science and Computing at Pentecost University, Sowutuom, Accra, Ghana.

---

## 1. Academic Structure & Department Overview

* **Institution**: Pentecost University, Accra, Ghana
* **Faculty**: Faculty of Engineering, Science and Computing (FESC)
* **Department**: Department of Information Technology
* **Program**: Bachelor of Science in Information Technology (B.Sc. IT)
* **Academic Level**: Level 400 Final Year Project
* **Format Authority**: Pentecost University *Project Work Manual* & *IT Project Template*

---

## 2. Departmental Word Count Requirements (Strict Constraints)

The Department of Information Technology enforces strict word count ranges. Deviations from these boundaries will trigger revision notices during departmental defense and grading.

| Document Section | Strict Word Count Range | Structural Constraint |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Executive Summary / Abstract** | **200 – 250 words** | **Strictly One (1) Single Paragraph** (No line breaks) |
| **Introduction Section Summary** | **40 – 50 words** | Concise statement of scope, problem, and primary contribution |
| **Chapter Summary** | **80 – 100 words** | Synthesis of chapter outcomes and forward link to next chapter |
| **Chapter One (Introduction)** | **800 – 1,000 words** | Background, Problem, Objectives, Scope, Limitations |
| **Chapter Two (Literature Review)** | **1,500 – 2,000 words** | Theoretical framework, review of related systems, research gaps |
| **Chapter Three (Methodology)** | **2,500 – 3,000 words** | RAD / Agile SDLC, data gathering instruments, requirements |
| **Chapter Four (System Analysis & Design)**| **2,000 – 2,500 words** | Use Cases, Sequence, DFD Level 0/1, ERD, Data Dictionary |
| **Chapter Five (Implementation & Evaluation)**| **2,000 – 2,500 words** | Architecture, testing, evaluation, usability trials, conclusion |

## 3. Formatting & Page Layout Specifications

* **Font Style & Size**:
  * Body Text: Times New Roman, 12 pt.
  * Chapter Headings (Heading 1): 14 pt, **Bold**, ALL CAPS, Centered (e.g., `CHAPTER ONE`, `EXECUTIVE SUMMARY`).
  * Subsection Headings (Heading 2): 12 pt, **Bold**, Sentence case, Left-aligned (e.g., `1.1 Background of the Study`).
  * Sub-subsection Headings (Heading 3): 12 pt, *Italic* or **Bold**, Sentence case.
* **Line Spacing**: 1.5 line spacing throughout the entire report body (single spacing permitted only inside data tables, source code listings, and block quotations).
* **Text Alignment**: Justified alignment for all narrative paragraphs.
* **Page Margins**:
  * **Left Margin**: **35 mm to 40 mm** (mandatory binding margin allowance).
  * **Right Margin**: **20 mm to 25 mm**.
  * **Top Margin**: **30 mm to 35 mm**.
  * **Bottom Margin**: **25 mm to 30 mm**.
* **Pagination**:
  * Preliminary pages (Declaration through Abbreviations): Lowercase Roman numerals (`i, ii, iii, iv...`), bottom-centered.
  * Body chapters (Chapter 1 onwards): Arabic numerals (`1, 2, 3...`), bottom-centered or top-right.

## 4. Preliminary Pages Sequence & Requirements

Every project report must include the following preliminary pages in exact chronological sequence:

1. **Cover / Title Page**:
   * Institutional header (Pentecost University, Faculty, Department).
   * Project Title: Centered, Bold, Title Case.
   * Student full name(s) and Student ID(s).
   * Degree qualification statement: *"A project report submitted to the Department of Information Technology..."*
   * Submission month and year.
2. **Declaration**:
   * Statement of originality and independent research.
   * Signature block with Student Name(s), Student ID(s), and Date.
   * Explicit OpenXML tab stops at 4.2 inches for alignment (never manual space padding).
3. **Certification / Supervisor Approval**:
   * Formal supervisor endorsement block with signature and date fields.
4. **Dedication**:
   * Brief dedication statement.
5. **Acknowledgements**:
   * Departmental, academic, institutional, and personal acknowledgements.
6. **Table of Contents**:
   * Complete hierarchy reflecting all Heading 1, 2, and 3 entries.
7. **List of Figures**:
   * Formatted table with figure numbers, captions, and page numbers.
8. **List of Tables**:
   * Formatted table with table numbers, titles, and page numbers.
9. **Abbreviations**:
   * Two-column acronym list using explicit 1.4-inch tab stop alignment.
10. **Executive Summary**:
    * Heading 1: `EXECUTIVE SUMMARY`.
    * Strictly **One (1) single paragraph**, strictly between **200 and 250 words**.

## 5. 5-Chapter Thesis Outline Blueprint

### Chapter One: Introduction (800 – 1,000 words)
* 1.1 Background of the Study (Broader industry context, technical domain evolution, and institutional significance).
* 1.2 Problem Statement (Precise technical or societal bottleneck addressed by the project).
* 1.3 Objectives of the Study:
  * General Objective (Primary purpose of the project).
  * Specific Objectives (3 to 5 measurable, actionable functional milestones).
* 1.4 Scope and Limitations (Platform, geographical boundaries, technological constraints, and excluded areas).
* 1.5 Significance of the Study (Benefits to industry, target users, academia, and local context).
* 1.6 Organization of the Study (Overview of chapter trajectories).
* 1.7 Chapter Summary (Strictly 80–100 words).

### Chapter Two: Literature Review (1,500 – 2,000 words)
* 2.1 Theoretical Framework (Foundational computational, mathematical, or architectural theories).
* 2.2 Review of the Domain / Industry Vector.
* 2.3 Review of Related Systems and Existing Solutions.
* 2.4 Comparative Technical Analysis (Feature comparison matrix highlighting shortcomings of existing systems).
* 2.5 Identification of Research Gaps & Proposed System Contribution.
* 2.6 Chapter Summary (80–100 words).

### Chapter Three: Methodology (2,500 – 3,000 words)
* 3.1 Research Design & SDLC Model (Rapid Application Development / Iterative Prototyping / Agile).
* 3.2 Requirements Elicitation Techniques (Interviews, surveys, document analysis, stakeholder observation).
* 3.3 Data Gathering Instruments & Sampling Strategy.
* 3.4 Data Integrity & Preprocessing Protocols (Validation, normalization, split strategies).
* 3.5 System Modeling & Evaluation Metrics (Precision, Recall, F1, Latency, Usability benchmarks).
* 3.6 Chapter Summary (80–100 words).

### Chapter Four: System Analysis and Design (2,000 – 2,500 words)
* 4.1 Functional and Non-Functional Requirements Specification.
* 4.2 Use Case Modeling (Actor descriptions, use case diagrams, narrative specifications).
* 4.3 Behavioral Modeling (Sequence diagrams detailing primary system transactions).
* 4.4 Data Flow Diagrams (Context Diagram / Level 0 DFD, Level 1 Process Decomposition).
* 4.5 Database Design (Entity-Relationship Diagram, relational schema, primary/foreign keys).
* 4.6 Data Dictionary (Table definitions, field types, nullability, constraints).
* 4.7 Chapter Summary (80–100 words).

### Chapter Five: System Implementation, Testing & Evaluation (2,000 – 2,500 words)
* 5.1 System Implementation Environment (Hardware specs, software development kit, programming languages, database).
* 5.2 Core Module Implementation (Code snippets, architectural wiring, service handlers).
* 5.3 System Testing:
  * Unit Testing (Test suites verifying individual functional units).
  * Integration Testing (End-to-end data pipelines and API workflows).
  * Empirical Performance Evaluation (Benchmark measurements, latency, throughput).
* 5.4 User Acceptance & Usability Evaluation (System Usability Scale [SUS] score distribution).
* 5.5 Limitations & Future Enhancements.
* 5.6 Recommendations & Conclusion.
* 5.7 Chapter Summary (80–100 words).

## 6. Citation & Reference Standards

* All references must strictly adhere to the **IEEE citation style**.
* In-text citations must use square brackets: `[1]`, `[2]`, `[3]–[5]`.
* Every listed reference must include complete author names, article title, publication journal or conference, volume, issue, page numbers, publication year, and an authentic **DOI** or direct URL.
* Speculative or hallucinated citations are strictly grounds for report rejection.
